Homicide refers to the act of one human being causing the death of another. This term encompasses both unlawful killings, such as murder and manslaughter, and lawful killings, such as those committed in self-defense or during wartime. The classification of a homicide depends on factors like intent, circumstances, and legal justifications. For instance, murder involves intentional and unlawful killing, whereas manslaughter pertains to unintentional killings resulting from recklessness or negligence. Justifiable homicides occur in situations where the killing is deemed legally permissible, such as in self-defense.

In the United States, homicide rates have experienced notable fluctuations over the past few years. Following a significant surge in homicides during the COVID-19 pandemic, recent data indicates a downward trend. In 2023, the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) reported an estimated 3% decrease in violent crime compared to the previous year, with murders and non-negligent manslaughter cases dropping by nearly 12%.

This decline continued into 2024, with analysts predicting a nearly 16% nationwide reduction in homicides, alongside a 3.3% drop in overall violent crime. Several major U.S. cities have contributed to this positive trend. Philadelphia, for example, recorded an all-time high of 562 homicides in 2021, which decreased to 516 in 2022 and 410 in 2023. By 2024, the city experienced a 40% reduction in homicides. Other cities, such as New Orleans, Washington, D.C., Memphis, Baltimore, Kansas City, and Los Angeles, also reported significant declines in homicide rates during the same period.

The factors contributing to these reductions are multifaceted. Community-based interventions, focused law enforcement strategies, and policy reforms have played crucial roles. The implementation of evidence-based approaches, such as those outlined in Thomas Abt's "Bleeding Out," has guided cities in addressing urban gun violence effectively. These strategies emphasize concentrating resources on high-risk individuals and areas, balancing enforcement with prevention, and ensuring fairness in legal processes.
